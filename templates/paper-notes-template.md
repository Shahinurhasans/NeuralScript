Copy this file, rename it, and replace each prompt in italics with your own writing. Explaining a paper is good practice; adding something of your own (a re-derivation, a small reproduction, or a case where the method fails) is what shows research judgment.

## TL;DR

_In two or three sentences: what the paper claims, and how convinced you are after reading it closely._

## The problem

_What were people trying to do, and why did earlier methods fall short? Define the notation you will use, for example a dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$._

## The key idea

_Explain the one insight that makes the method work. Derive it where you can; a careful derivation is often the most useful part of a post like this._

## What the evidence shows

_Which experiments support the claim, and which are weaker than the abstract suggests? Note the baselines, datasets, and compute budget._

## Reproducing one result

_Re-run one small experiment. Report what matched, what didn't, and link your code._

## Open questions

_What would you try next, and why? This section shows how you think as a researcher, so make it specific._

## References

1. _Author, A., & Author, B. (Year). Title of the paper. Venue. https://arxiv.org/abs/0000.00000_
